# GTM, Content, and Partnerships Implementation

## Outcome

Win one fully funded sponsor program through a narrow founder-led B2B motion,
then use delivered operating evidence to earn repeat sponsors, distribution
partners, and credible ecosystem attention. Content makes the product easier to
understand; it does not manufacture traction or lead with the token.

## Positioning

### Buyer-facing category

> Sponsor-funded health protection for international cohorts.

### Buyer promise

> Give your members a clear, funded health safety net for a defined program,
> with private evidence, accountable decisions, and verifiable settlement.

### Company vision

> Nakama is building infrastructure for community-owned, agent-operated
> protection—beginning with one sponsor-funded cohort.

### Technology explanation

> The Nakama Operator reduces program administration. Robinhood Chain records
> funding, authority, and settlement. Sensitive health evidence remains
> encrypted offchain. Virtuals is the planned agent-commerce and conditional
> tokenization route.

Do not lead sponsor conversations with autonomous mutuals, RWA, tokenomics,
ACF, or market cap. These may matter after the buyer confirms the current
problem, budget, and operating need.

## ICP and Account Qualification

### Primary ICP

A named legal operator with:

- repeated international cohorts of 50–250 people, or a 500–5,000-member
  organization with comparable exposure
- an upcoming program inside 90 days
- prior health incidents, emergency support, insurance friction, or member
  safety complaints
- paid membership, tuition, sponsorship, event, residency, employment, or
  treasury economics
- authority to fund a complete program and pay Nakama's fees
- a verifiable roster/eligibility process
- willingness to provide data, member access, and accountable operators

### Likely segments

- global residencies and fellowship programs
- remote-work and creator communities with paid programs
- accelerators, bootcamps, and founder schools running international cohorts
- conferences, retreats, and hackathons with a responsible organizer
- membership organizations with mobile members and existing assistance spend
- employers or workforce platforms with defined short-term international groups

Network School can be researched as one potential account context, but recent
controversy is not a marketing asset. Nakama should never exploit member harm,
imply a partnership, or pursue controversy-driven buzz. The test is whether a
named operator has authority, need, budget, and willingness to protect members.

### Disqualifiers

- audience/community with no accountable entity or member roster
- no prior incident, deadline, current spend, or budget path
- desire for unlimited/global/retroactive coverage
- request to announce before terms and funding
- interest driven primarily by token allocation or publicity
- inability to provide lawful member data and communications
- geography or product structure counsel cannot support

## Founder-Led Sales Motion

### Stage 1 — Account research

For each account record:

- entity, program, decision-maker, cohort size/dates/geography
- revenue/treasury/sponsorship model
- public evidence of health/safety incidents or current benefits
- current alternative and likely cost owner
- warm path and credibility anchor
- legal/privacy/geography flags
- specific reason the next 90 days could matter

No outreach claims private knowledge, diagnoses a problem from controversy, or
states that an adjacent community is already a customer.

### Stage 2 — Discovery

The first call answers:

1. What happened in the last two cohorts when a member needed care?
2. What did the member, team, and sponsor do, pay, or risk?
3. Which existing insurance, assistance, reimbursement, or informal process was
   used, and where did it fail?
4. Which future cohort creates a deadline?
5. Who owns the member promise, budget, legal risk, and operating decision?
6. What data, payment, and next meeting will the buyer commit if Nakama can
   produce a viable design?

The call does not ask whether the buyer likes blockchain. It tests a costly
problem and operating authority.

### Stage 3 — Protection Design Sprint

Offer a fixed-scope paid sprint, initially `$2,500–$10,000` as a hypothesis,
that produces:

- cohort and incident/current-alternative analysis
- eligible member and geography map
- bounded benefit/exclusion/cap options
- full-budget scenarios and unused-fund behavior
- legal, privacy, partner, and operational question register
- member and sponsor workflows
- implementation plan, economics, and go/no-go recommendation

Discount only in exchange for explicit value: useful data, representative
member access, faster decision, case-study rights, or strategic introduction.
A free bespoke sprint trains the market to value Nakama as unpaid research.

### Stage 4 — Funded pilot proposal

The proposal separates:

- Nakama design/implementation fee
- recurring or cohort administration fee
- complete sponsor program budget
- pass-through legal, reviewer, partner, custody, KYC, gas, and support costs
- sponsor responsibilities and decision timeline
- member activation and service commitments
- gates that can cancel or delay launch
- closure, unused-budget, renewal, and case-study terms

Only signed scope plus collected cash advances the revenue stage. A logo,
verbal yes, memorandum, token interest, or friendly introduction does not.

### Stage 5 — Renewal and referral

The closure meeting presents operations, budget, member behavior, incidents,
agent performance, economics, limitations, and corrective actions. Ask for one
of three costly outcomes:

- next cohort at agreed scope/price
- expansion to another program or member group
- introduction to a comparable budget owner

Two of the first three sponsors must renew, expand, or refer for early
behavior-change evidence.

## Pipeline Targets

### First 30 days

- 40 named qualified accounts
- 30 decision-maker interviews
- at least 8 top-three problem confirmations
- at least 3 useful data shares
- 10 priced design proposals
- at least 2 paid design partners
- one preferred Genesis sponsor candidate

### Pipeline hygiene

CRM stages follow the definitions in the metrics plan. Each opportunity records
authority, incident, current alternative, spend, deadline, cohort, geography,
legal risk, next costly commitment, amount, probability evidence, and exact
blocker. Social followers and token-channel members are not sponsor pipeline.

## Channel Strategy

### Direct founder network

Use the founder's doctor-builder identity, two years of sustained work, health
product/protocol experience, relevant ecosystem proximity, and community-
builder network to earn a serious first conversation. These establish
credibility; they do not replace customer evidence.

### Licensed and service partners

Potential partners include insurers, TPAs, medical-assistance networks,
telehealth/care navigation, payment/off-ramp providers, custody, stablecoin
infrastructure, KYC/identity, actuarial/risk, privacy, and legal specialists.

The partner pitch is:

> Nakama originates and operates bounded international cohorts with an
> agent-assisted workflow and verifiable economic state. We need a qualified
> partner for the specific licensed/service responsibility, with clear data,
> authority, and economics.

Do not ask a regulated partner to endorse an undefined token project. Bring the
exact sponsor, cohort, terms, budget, workflow, and responsibility map.

### Cohort and membership platforms

If direct ACV or sales cycle fails, sell through organizations already serving
many cohorts. A platform/TPA can make Nakama a reusable operating layer rather
than repeated founder-led custom sales.

### Virtuals ecosystem

Engage Virtuals with:

- a working Robinhood-native agent and testnet product
- one-page product/agent architecture
- public non-PHI ACP job and evaluation
- sponsor demand evidence that can be shared
- exact token-necessity and separation explanation
- founder background and sustained execution history
- written questions on eligibility, mechanics, support, and public wording

Ask for technical validation, ACP integration feedback, written platform
answers, appropriate introductions, and the process for curation or launch
support. Do not ask the team to pump the token or imply that approval guarantees
market performance.

### Robinhood Chain ecosystem

Engage the chain team with a verified testnet demo, architecture, AA/member UX,
USDG questions, production infrastructure needs, public reserve use case, and
factual brand-language request. Ask separately about technical support, RWA or
ecosystem review, grants, partners, and distribution; none is implied by
deployment.

## Partnership Diligence Package

Maintain a shareable, versioned packet:

1. one-page company, current product, and long-term vision
2. founder and team credibility
3. exact sponsor ICP and problem evidence
4. Genesis product terms and member journey
5. agent inputs, outputs, permissions, and evaluations
6. Robinhood architecture and verified testnet addresses
7. privacy, legal, stablecoin, security, and trust boundaries
8. business model and observed/estimated unit economics
9. token role, non-role, conditional gate, and current platform questions
10. requested partnership action and what Nakama provides in return

Every number states source, period, denominator, and evidence level. Private
sponsor or member information is excluded unless disclosure is authorized.

## Content System

### Content pillars

1. **The accepted failure:** what international cohort operators do when
   members face an acute health issue and formal coverage does not fit.
2. **The complete program:** terms, budget, privacy, decisions, appeals, and
   settlement, explained without crypto jargon.
3. **The productive agent:** real tasks, artifacts, evaluations, human
   authority, and time saved.
4. **Public economic truth:** what onchain funding can verify and what it cannot.
5. **Building responsibly:** legal unknowns, kill criteria, incidents, and why
   the token is separate.
6. **The future category:** how repeatable sponsor programs could become
   community-owned autonomous mutuals.

### Evidence ladder for content

Publish in this order:

- problem interviews and anonymized patterns with permission
- design artifacts and interactive prototype
- testnet lifecycle and verified addresses
- agent evaluation and paid ACP job
- signed sponsor fact only with permission
- funded mainnet program and exact public state
- measured program outcome and renewal
- token mechanics only after verified creation

The content calendar cannot outrun this ladder.

- accountable agent/human roles

### Sponsor deck

1. Cohort operator's current failure and cost
2. Why current alternatives leave a gap
3. Sponsor-funded Genesis Protection Program
4. Member journey and promise
5. Budget, cap, and operating model
6. Nakama Operator and measured task value
7. Trust, privacy, decisions, and appeals
8. Robinhood Chain role and limitations
9. Pilot scope and economics
10. Evidence, open questions, and decision timeline
11. Long-term autonomous mutual infrastructure
12. Specific ask

Do not put tokenomics before the customer offer.

### Product demo

The seven-minute demo should show:

1. sponsor describes the cohort and current problem
2. agent creates a bounded proposal with sources and uncertainty
3. human reviews terms, budget, and maximum liability
4. sponsor funds testnet USDG and sees reconciliation
5. member activates without ETH or `$NAKAMA`
6. synthetic private evidence enters review and appeal
7. exact settlement and public-safe report are verified

### Technical paper

Explain trust planes, immutable program suite, budget invariant, private
evidence commitment, typed decisions, AA policy, finality, dependency risk,
agent authorization, and progressive decentralization. It should be useful to
an auditor and partner, not a substitute for customer copy.

### Trust center

Publish current legal/product status, privacy architecture, subprocessors where
appropriate, security/reporting process, stablecoin and chain dependencies,
agent limitations, incident contact, verified deployments, and current audits.
Planned certifications or legal structures stay labelled planned.

### Agent launch materials

- agent name and one productive job
- exact input/output example using synthetic/public data
- tool and data boundaries
- human approval and evaluation
- ACP listing and price
- Robinhood network evidence
- support and incident route
- tokenless status or exact verified token link

### Conditional token materials

Created in draft but remain unpublished until the token gate:

- exact tokenomics and allocation diagram
- mechanics and risk page
- treasury and use-of-funds policy
- product/token separation diagram
- launch agreement and restrictions
- verified address anti-scam kit
- launch and incident FAQ

> international cohorts. We help operators define a bounded program, fund it
> upfront, handle evidence privately, and make settlement auditable. I’d like to
> understand how your team handles acute member incidents today; if the gap is

> Doctor-builder in KL, two years into Nakama: agent-operated, sponsor-funded
> health protection for global cohorts, with private evidence and public
> settlement. We’re building Robinhood-native and evaluating Virtuals/ACP. I’d
> value a short technical and eligibility conversation once the demo is ready.

This note opens a relationship. The follow-up should bring the working demo and
specific questions, not a request for amplification.

## GTM Experiments

| Experiment | Hypothesis | Costly signal | Pass/fail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paid design sprint | Operators pay to define a viable program before build | Collected fee/deposit | At least 2 of 10 qualified proposals |
| Incident-data request | Urgent buyers will share useful aggregate history | Data under appropriate process | At least 3 of 30 interviews |
| Sponsor prototype | Buyer can understand and approve a bounded proposal | Completes review without founder reinterpretation | 4 of 5 representative buyers |
| Member onboarding | Sponsor-funded members trust and understand the flow | Activation and comprehension | At least 70% activation, 80% comprehension |
| Public reserve proof | Onchain verification affects buyer/member trust | Stated preference plus behavior | Evidence from pilot interviews/use |
| ACP listing | External users pay for a bounded Nakama capability | Accepted paid production job | At least one before utility claim |
| Partner channel | Aggregator can bring multiple qualified cohorts | Qualified co-sell pipeline or contract | Compare ACV/cycle/cost with direct |

## Practical GTM Vetoes

- do not use health emergencies or controversy as launch entertainment
- do not announce a sponsor, partner, team relationship, approval, or grant
  without written permission
- do not mix legacy app/token audiences into Genesis traction
- do not buy fake engagement, undisclosed promotion, volume, or testimonials
- do not compensate referrals or content in ways that violate legal/ethical
  duties or remain undisclosed
- do not let a token community contact or pressure members/reviewers
- do not set a token date before exact platform/legal gates
- do not publish synthetic demos as real programs
- do not promise the first cohort before full funding and activation

## GTM Review Cadence

Weekly review asks:

- Which costly customer behavior occurred?
- Which repeated problem and current alternative became clearer?
- Where did authority, budget, legal structure, or trust fail?
- Which content created qualified buyer conversations rather than vanity reach?
- Which partner shortened or complicated the path?
- What will be stopped this week to focus on the highest-value proof?

If the only positive graph is social or token attention, the GTM motion has not
worked.
