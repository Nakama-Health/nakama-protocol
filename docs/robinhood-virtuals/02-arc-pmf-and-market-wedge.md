# Arc PMF Diagnosis and Market Wedge

## Verdict

Nakama is a **Future Vision** company addressing a **Hard Fact** through a
targeted **Hair-on-Fire** beachhead. The long-term proposition—that communities
can operate trustworthy protection networks with agents and public settlement—
requires a new category and substantial belief change. The initial buyer must
already feel an urgent, budgeted problem so Nakama can reach commercial proof
before the entire category matures.

The largest unresolved risk is not technical feasibility. It is whether a
community operator will commit reputation, members, reserve capital, and money
to a new protection model, and whether members will actually enroll and behave
differently.

## Archetype Diagnosis

| Path | Fit | Customer relationship | Evidence today | Operating implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Future Vision | High | Most buyers do not yet believe a small community can safely create its own protection system | The thesis is coherent, but no completed autonomous mutual proves it | Collapse disbelief with one complete funded cohort and a public post-cohort report |
| Hard Fact | Medium | Global workers and communities may accept fragmented coverage, exclusions, reimbursement friction, and emergency crowdfunding as normal | Existing research is directional; resignation, frequency, and budget displacement have not been measured in the target ICP | Name the accepted failure in buyer language and prove its frequency and current cost |
| Hair on Fire | Low in the general market; potentially high in the beachhead | A paid cohort operator with an upcoming program, prior incidents, and an existing safety budget may have a deadline-driven problem | The exact segment, incident history, and budget owner remain unverified | Sell only to operators with a bounded upcoming cohort, prior incidents, and authority over a real budget |

This hybrid should not be averaged into one message. The company story is
Future Vision. The sales offer solves a time-bound Hair-on-Fire problem. The
product experience demonstrates that the Hard Fact is changeable.

## Right to Exist

Nakama earns a right to exist only if it combines insights a generic software
or insurance team would miss:

- communities are underused distribution and trust networks
- small protection pools fail because administration, capital, and verification
  have high fixed costs
- agents can compress those costs but require bounded authority
- public settlement is valuable for shared economic truth, not for raw medical
  data
- a protocol can standardize pools without making every risk identical

The founder story strengthens this answer when it remains specific: a doctor-
builder who has spent two years working across health software, claims,
protocols, and community protection. It weakens when longevity is presented as
market proof or when unrelated product history is added to inflate traction.

### Right-to-exist test

Interview:

- five global community or residency operators
- five buyers familiar with travel, benefits, or member protection
- three licensed insurance, TPA, mutual, or captive professionals
- two skeptical crypto or agent-economy operators

The thesis advances only if at least five qualified operators identify the same
status-quo failure and at least three want to help shape a concrete pool.

## Do People Care Enough?

The first ICP is:

> A named legal operator running repeated international cohorts of 50–250
> people, or a 500–5,000-member organization with the same exposure, prior
> health incidents, an upcoming program within 90 days, and authority over a
> complete sponsor-funded protection budget.

Positive attributes:

- members travel or live across multiple countries
- the community already provides benefits, grants, stipends, or emergency help
- leadership can contact and verify members
- an upcoming cohort creates a deadline within 90 days
- the community has a treasury or paid membership model
- leadership cares about retention, safety, or institutional credibility

Disqualifiers:

- an audience with no membership or operator accountability
- no ability to identify eligible members
- no budget owner or unwillingness to fund the complete Phase 0 program
- a desire for unlimited or retroactive protection
- a buyer seeking marketing theater rather than operating responsibility
- a jurisdiction or activity that counsel cannot support

### Commitment ladder

Interest is scored by costly behavior:

1. shares cohort and incident data
2. introduces the actual decision-maker
3. schedules a terms workshop
4. signs a design-partner letter
5. pays a deposit or design fee
6. commits the complete program budget and operating backstop
7. recruits eligible members

Only steps five through seven count as strong demand evidence.

## Does the Product Change Behavior?

The product must change behavior for both buyer and member.

The operator changes from informal emergency support or fragmented vendor
coordination to a pre-funded, explicit program. The member changes from waiting
until a crisis to enrolling in advance, understanding terms, and submitting
evidence through a defined process.

### Activation event

A community is activated when:

- terms are approved
- the complete sponsor program budget is posted
- at least the minimum cohort has enrolled
- all operating roles are staffed
- the launch window begins

A member is activated when:

- eligibility is verified
- terms are acknowledged
- the sponsor-funded member allocation is confirmed
- a membership commitment is recorded

Wallet connection, token purchase, waitlist signup, and app download are not
product activation.

### Retention event

The strongest early retention event is the operator choosing to run another
cohort, expand the member count, or introduce another qualified community after
reviewing the first report. For members, retention is re-enrollment when the
next relevant window opens.

## Will Customers Pay Enough?

Nakama should test a B2B2C model:

- the community operator pays for design, launch, and administration
- the sponsor funds the complete Phase 0 program budget according to published
  terms; member contributions are a separately gated later model
- specialist agent and evaluator work is priced explicitly
- claims-paying reserve is segregated from Nakama operating revenue

Initial pricing is a hypothesis, not a published promise:

| Component | Test range | What it proves |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Design-partner deposit | $2,500–$10,000 | Whether the problem merits budget before custom work |
| Pool launch fee | $10,000–$25,000 | Whether configuration, legal coordination, deployment, and onboarding create material buyer value |
| Administration | $3–$10 per activated member per month or an equivalent cohort fee | Whether recurring operations can support software and accountable review |
| Agent jobs | Fixed published price by job | Whether external users or agents value specific Nakama capabilities |

These ranges must be tested in discovery. They are not revenue projections.
Claims capital, sponsor contributions, taxes, licensed-partner economics, and
pass-through service costs are separate.

## Commercial Pit Stop

The commercial pit stop is not the token launch. It is a paid **Protection
Design Sprint** for one qualified community.

The sprint produces:

- cohort definition and eligibility map
- incident and current-alternative analysis
- benefit and exclusion proposal
- contribution, reserve, and exposure scenarios
- jurisdiction and partner questions
- member flow and communications
- operating roles and service levels
- deployment and launch recommendation

The output has independent value even if the pool does not launch. That allows
Nakama to earn, learn, and qualify buyers before assuming exposure.

## Thirty-Day PMF Program

1. **Run concentrated discovery.** Contact 40 qualified operators, hold at least
   15 calls, and capture the current alternative, incident frequency, budget
   owner, deadline, member count, and next commitment in a structured record.
2. **Sell three design sprints.** Ask for money before custom protocol work.
   Discounting is acceptable only in exchange for data, member access, public
   case-study rights, or another explicit asset.
3. **Test the magic moment.** Put an interactive agent prototype in front of
   five operators and measure whether they can produce and understand a pool
   proposal without founder interpretation.
4. **Select one Genesis partner.** Choose based on urgency, authority, data,
   legal feasibility, reserve capability, and willingness to publish results—
   not social reach alone.

## Self-Deception Risks

- Two years of work proves persistence, not demand.
- Existing health-app usage does not prove willingness to buy protection.
- A reservation, wallet connection, or token purchase does not prove product
  behavior change.
- A large community following is not a usable cohort without authority,
  eligibility, and member access.
- Virtuals attention is not design-partner interest.
- A community saying it would participate for free does not validate pricing.
- A launch sponsored entirely by Nakama may test operations but cannot prove
  customer willingness to pay.
- One successful cohort can prove the wedge; it does not validate every type of
  autonomous mutual.
