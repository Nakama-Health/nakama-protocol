# Go-to-Market and Launch

## GTM Thesis

Nakama should not begin with a public consumer funnel. The product needs a
responsible sponsor, a defined cohort, approved terms, and posted capital, so
the first motion is founder-led B2B2C sales to community operators.

The token launch creates a second, parallel funnel of builders, supporters,
agents, and potential capital partners. It does not replace the buyer funnel.

## Beachhead ICP

Target organizations that satisfy all of the following:

- a legal entity and named decision-maker
- repeated international cohorts of 50–250 people or 500–5,000 members
- recurring program fees, membership dues, sponsorship, or treasury budget
- prior health incidents, emergency grants, cancellations, or assistance work
- a bounded upcoming cohort within 90 days
- ability to verify eligibility and communicate directly with members
- willingness to post the complete pilot budget
- willingness to operate under clear terms, privacy rules, and reporting

Initial account types:

- founder residencies and paid cohort programs
- remote-work or global-talent communities
- accelerators and ecosystem programs with international gatherings
- membership organizations with recurring travel
- conferences or hackathons with a responsible legal operator and sponsor

"Digital nomads" is a member description, not an ICP. DAOs, crypto communities,
and Network School-adjacent groups qualify only when the actual operator,
entity, budget, and authority are known.

## Entry Offer

Sell a Protection Design Sprint before selling a protocol deployment. The sprint
creates value, qualifies the customer, and prevents free bespoke consulting.

### Discovery call outcome

Every call should end with one of four states:

- disqualified with a reason
- follow-up with the actual decision-maker
- paid design-sprint proposal
- no current fit but a dated trigger for re-engagement

"Interested" is not a pipeline stage.

### Discovery questions

- Tell me about the last two member health incidents your team had to handle.
- What did the organization pay, reimburse, refund, or coordinate?
- Who spent time on the case, and how many hours?
- What protection do members currently have, and where does it fail?
- What cohort or travel window is coming next?
- Who can approve a program and program budget?
- Would the organization fund the program, charge members, or seek a sponsor?
- What proof would make you renew after the first cohort?
- What would prevent legal, finance, or community approval?

Do not start by asking whether the prospect likes crypto or autonomous mutuals.

## Account Scoring

Score each account from zero to two on:

- urgency within 90 days
- incident history
- budget owner access
- existing support spend
- cohort/member identifiability
- legal entity and accountable operator
- ability to post program budget
- willingness to provide data
- willingness to publish a case study
- strategic fit with the first jurisdiction and product template

Accounts scoring below 14/20 should not receive custom engineering.

## Funnel Targets

### Days 1–30

- 40 qualified accounts researched
- 30 high-quality founder-led outreaches
- 15 buyer conversations
- 8 actual decision-makers
- 5 proposals
- 3 paid or deposit-backed design partners

### Days 31–60

- complete three design sprints
- select one Genesis sponsor
- secure full pilot-budget commitment
- approve one product and jurisdiction structure
- obtain a named launch date and recruitment plan

### Days 61–90

- activate the first cohort or satisfy every gate except a documented external
  legal/capital dependency
- enroll at least 50 eligible members
- run the full private evidence and decision flow with real eligible cases or
  documented tabletop cases when none occur
- obtain sponsor renewal intent and member feedback

## Channel Strategy

### Founder-led direct sales

This is the primary early channel because the product is trust-sensitive and
the category requires education. The founder should use doctor-builder
credibility to reach the decision-maker, then rely on a concrete program
proposal rather than personal reputation to close.

### Referral partners

Potential partners include:

- residency and cohort platforms
- global mobility providers
- benefits consultants
- travel assistance and claims administrators
- crypto ecosystem foundations
- accelerator networks
- licensed insurers, TPAs, captives, or mutual operators

Partners become scalable channels only after one direct program works.

### Virtuals ecosystem

Use Virtuals to reach:

- productive agent builders
- ACP service buyers and providers
- token-aligned early supporters
- potential protocol contributors
- ecosystem partners who can verify or amplify shipped work

Do not treat token traders as the primary sponsor ICP.

### Robinhood Chain ecosystem

Use the Robinhood Chain developer ecosystem for:

- technical validation
- infrastructure and wallet partners
- stablecoin, oracle, bridge, indexing, and security relationships
- buildathons or ecosystem programs
- eventual RWA reserve integrations when a real asset structure exists

Deployment does not create brokerage distribution. Any wallet, app, listing, or
co-marketing path requires its own written agreement.

## Design-Partner Contract

A design partner should commit:

- paid fee or non-trivial deposit
- access to a named decision-maker
- de-identified incident and current-workflow data
- cohort size, dates, locations, and eligibility inputs
- weekly working session during design
- a decision deadline
- pilot budget source
- member recruitment responsibility
- permission level for public attribution or anonymized case study

Nakama should commit:

- fixed deliverables and timeline
- privacy and data-use boundaries
- a go/no-go recommendation rather than a forced launch
- transparent pass-through costs
- no public use of the partner's name without written approval
- credit or refund behavior stated in advance

## Virtuals Launch Sequencing

The recommended launch sequence is evidence-first:

### T-minus 8 to 6 weeks

- close at least one paid design partner
- make the Nakama Operator produce useful scenario outputs
- publish the category thesis and product boundaries
- engage Virtuals team members with a working demo and diligence packet
- confirm the current Robinhood launch route in writing

### T-minus 6 to 4 weeks

- deploy and verify the complete testnet product loop
- publish contract architecture and threat model
- launch at least one paid agent service
- freeze token utility and treasury policies
- record the founder video and prepare launch materials

### T-minus 4 to 2 weeks

- complete independent contract and launch review
- publish team identities, vesting, token limitations, and use-of-funds policy
- run community Q&A around the product, not price
- demonstrate the member experience without requiring `$NAKAMA`

### T-minus 2 weeks to TGE

- create and schedule the Virtuals launch with adequate review time
- verify every immutable field, wallet, allocation, vest, and fee setting
- publish the live proof dashboard
- rehearse incident, moderation, support, and market-integrity responses
- keep claims-program funds and token-market wallets segregated

### First 60 days after TGE

- ship weekly product evidence
- report agent jobs and customer milestones separately from token metrics
- deliver the Genesis sponsor program gates
- publish treasury receipts and material roadmap deviations
- avoid roadmap inflation in response to short-term price movement

## Launch Quality Gate

Nakama should not launch solely because the interface permits it. Minimum
launch quality requires:

- one paid design partner
- a working Robinhood testnet flow from program creation through a test payout
- a live productive agent with at least one paid or independently useful job
- public team and founder identity
- clear token utility and non-utility
- signed-off token allocation and wallet register
- legal review of token and product communications
- contract review and operational incident plan
- evidence dashboard and public roadmap
- written confirmation of any Virtuals/Robinhood-specific mechanics not covered
  by current official documentation

## Sales and Token Community Separation

Maintain separate systems:

- CRM and buyer pipeline for sponsors
- member enrollment and support for protected cohorts
- developer/agent pipeline for ACP and protocol contributors
- token community channels for launch and governance information

Never route a member claim through a public token Discord or require public
posting for support. Never count token-channel growth as sponsor pipeline.

## Partnership Strategy

### Virtuals

Approach the team with:

- a 60-second live agent demonstration
- the full product and token separation
- one paid design-partner signal
- the current testnet contract architecture
- specific requests: launch-mechanic confirmation, ACP onboarding, Butler
  discovery, team review, and Kuala Lumpur meeting

The request is collaboration and diligence, not a guarantee that the team will
promote or support price.

### Robinhood Chain

Approach the developer ecosystem with:

- a concrete financial application and testnet deployment
- account-abstraction and stablecoin needs
- explicit questions about asset support, finality, bridges, oracles, custody,
  indexing, and incident escalation
- no implication that the brokerage or app endorses the product

### Design partners

Lead with prior incident cost and the next cohort deadline. Mention the chain
and agent after the operating problem is understood.

## GTM Failure Modes

- using public launch activity to avoid direct customer rejection
- choosing a famous community with no buyer or budget
- free pilots with no conversion terms
- over-customizing every cohort
- promising broad global availability before one operating corridor works
- marketing autonomous claims before decision quality is proven
- using controversy for reach in a trust-sensitive category
- announcing partnerships from exploratory conversations
- subsidizing every program with token treasury funds and calling it revenue

## GTM Kill Criteria

- Fewer than 8 of 30 qualified operators rank the problem among their top three:
  reject or narrow the ICP.
- Fewer than 2 of 10 fixed proposals become paid design partnerships: stop
  building community-specific features.
- No buyer commits both operating fees and the complete pilot budget: the
  current offer does not create enough value.
- Fewer than 70% of eligible sponsor-funded members activate: remove product
  friction and retest whether the benefit matters.
- Fewer than 2 of the first 3 sponsors renew or expand: the product has not
  changed buyer behavior.
- Every third program still needs more than 20 hours of bespoke setup or custom
  contracts: the platform thesis is not yet true.
