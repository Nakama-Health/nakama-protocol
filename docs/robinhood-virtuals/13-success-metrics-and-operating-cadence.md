# Success Metrics and Operating Cadence

## Measurement Principle

Nakama must prove a customer business before interpreting token activity as
success. Product, operations, protocol, agent, and token metrics are separate
scoreboards because each answers a different question.

The Phase 0 north-star metric is:

> **Fully funded protected member-days completed within published service and
> safety terms.**

This combines real sponsor commitment, member activation, time at risk,
adequate funding, and operational delivery. It does not increase when someone
buys `$NAKAMA`, follows an account, connects a wallet, or joins a waitlist.

The long-term category metric can become renewed protected member-months after
Nakama proves repeated programs. Counting exposure before terms, funding, and
activation would inflate the denominator with imaginary protection.

## Evidence Levels

Every dashboard value and public number carries an evidence level.

| Level | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E0 — assertion | Said by a founder, prospect, or document without reviewable proof | "Communities need this" |
| E1 — observed | Directly observed behavior, interview, or system event | Decision-maker shares incident data |
| E2 — committed | A costly action or signed obligation | Paid design sprint or sponsor budget deposited |
| E3 — delivered | Product performed the agreed job with traceable records | Activated cohort and settled approved request |
| E4 — repeated | The same buyer repeats, expands, or a second comparable buyer succeeds | Sponsor renewal and second cohort |
| E5 — independently verified | External reviewer can reproduce or attest the result | Audited vault accounting or approved case study |

No public copy promotes an E0 or E1 statement as traction. Internal metrics
identify source, owner, timestamp, denominator, definition version, and whether
the result can be published.

## PMF Scoreboard

### Problem and segment evidence

| Metric | Definition | Initial gate | Decision it drives |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Qualified interviews | Interview with ICP decision-maker covering incident, current alternative, budget, authority, and deadline | 30 | Whether the segment pattern is real |
| Top-three problem rate | Qualified interviews ranking the problem among their three most urgent program risks | At least 8 of 30 | Continue or narrow the ICP |
| Data-sharing rate | Buyers sharing useful incident, cohort, or current-spend data under an appropriate process | At least 3 of 30 | Whether urgency survives a costly request |
| Repeated current alternative | Same workaround or vendor failure named by independent buyers | One dominant pattern across at least 5 | Product and narrative focus |
| Upcoming-window rate | Qualified buyers with a cohort/program inside 90 days | Track by segment | Whether the wedge is deadline driven |

### Willingness to pay

| Metric | Definition | Initial gate | Decision it drives |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Paid design partners | Executed scope plus collected design fee or non-refundable deposit | At least 2 of first 10 qualified proposals | Continue bespoke discovery work |
| Full-pilot funding | Sponsor contracts and deposits the complete approved program budget | 1 | Build funded production path |
| Price realization | Contracted Nakama fees divided by list-price hypothesis | Track without hidden discounts | Packaging and buyer quality |
| Sales cycle | Days from qualified first call to collected payment | Target under 90 days | Direct versus channel GTM |
| Expected ACV | Contracted implementation plus administration, excluding program budget | Target at least about $15,000 | Whether direct sales can support delivery |

### Behavior change and retention

| Metric | Definition | Initial gate | Decision it drives |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Sponsor activation | Terms approved, full budget funded, roles assigned, cohort opened | 100% before any member promise | Truth of launch |
| Member activation | Eligible members completing identity, terms, and program activation | At least 70% of eligible sponsor-funded cohort | Trust and UX fit |
| Pre-program comprehension | Members correctly answering scope, cap, exclusions, evidence, and appeal questions | At least 80% | Copy and onboarding readiness |
| Appropriate use | Requests matching stated program intent versus avoidable support confusion | Baseline then improve | Terms and member education |
| Sponsor renewal/expansion | Sponsor runs another cohort or expands paid scope | At least 2 of first 3 sponsors | Behavior-change proof |
| Qualified referral | Sponsor introduces a comparable decision-maker who reaches a proposal | Track | Trust and repeatable distribution |

## Product and Service Scoreboard

| Metric | Definition | Phase 0 target |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Time to configured proposal | Complete sponsor input to reviewable terms, budget model, and operating plan | Same working session for standard template; under 1 business day final draft |
| Time to launch | Signed sponsor scope to activation-ready program | Establish baseline; third program under 20 hours of custom staff work |
| Evidence completeness first pass | Requests requiring no second round for predictable missing evidence | At least 80% after onboarding stabilization |
| Clean-case decision time | Evidence-ready to signed determination excluding approved pauses | Under 48 hours |
| Urgent acknowledgement | Urgent tagged request to accountable human acknowledgement | Defined by program; tested continuously |
| Appeal rate | Appealed determinations divided by appeal-eligible determinations | Under 15%, investigated rather than optimized blindly |
| Material decision error | Determinations changed due to reviewer/system error, excluding legitimate new evidence | Under 2% of determinations |
| Settlement success | Final approved obligations settled exactly once to correct recipient | 100% |
| Support resolution | Member questions resolved within published service level | At least 95% |
| Privacy complaints | Substantiated privacy/process complaints | Zero severe; all tracked and closed |
| Member trust | Post-program answer to "I would rely on this program again" | Baseline, target at least 70% positive |

Low use is not automatically success or failure. It may mean the cohort had no
eligible incidents, members did not trust the product, or terms were irrelevant.
The post-program review separates exposure outcome from behavior and service
quality.

## Economic Scoreboard

Program budget, Nakama revenue, token treasury, and ACP economics are reported
separately.

### Customer economics

- contracted design and launch fees
- recurring administration fee
- pass-through legal, reviewer, KYC, custody, gas, and support costs
- direct implementation and operating labor
- contribution margin before token or creator-fee income
- time to collect and days of working capital
- renewal, expansion, and support burden

### Program economics

- sponsor budget received
- maximum liability and encumbered amount
- approved obligations and settlements
- unused budget under the signed return/reuse policy
- direct service costs paid from the correct account
- stablecoin price/liquidity exceptions

### Unit-economic gates

The direct sponsor motion remains viable only if the first repeatable package
supports roughly `$15,000+` annual or cohort-level Nakama revenue, closes inside
about 90 days, and trends toward positive contribution margin without more than
20 hours of custom staff setup by the third program. These are decision
thresholds, not forecasts or public price promises.

If buyers care but direct economics fail, move upstream to a licensed partner,
assistance network, TPA, membership platform, or cohort aggregator rather than
hiding services labor inside token revenue.

## Agent Scoreboard

The Nakama Operator earns a place in the product only if it reduces cost or
improves reliability under bounded authority.

| Metric | Definition | Gate |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Valid proposal rate | Generated proposals passing deterministic schema and policy checks | At least 95% for approved template |
| Human acceptance | Recommendations accepted without material correction | Baseline by task, then improve |
| Material override | Human changes terms, amount, eligibility, or decision reasoning | Track by cause; never hide |
| Unsupported assertion | Output containing a material claim without a cited approved source | Zero in final customer output |
| Unauthorized tool attempt | Attempt outside role, selector, value, data class, or program | Zero successful; all blocked and reviewed |
| Sensitive-data leakage | Restricted data in logs, prompts outside policy, output, or ACP | Zero |
| Staff time saved | Controlled comparison against manual workflow for the same task | Positive and measured before autonomy expands |
| Recommendation latency | Ready input to structured recommendation | Task-specific SLA |
| ACP paid production jobs | External paid jobs delivered and accepted | At least 1 before token utility claim; repeat use before marketplace claim |
| ACP dispute/refund rate | Disputed or refunded jobs divided by completed jobs | Track with root cause |

High acceptance can mean rubber-stamping rather than model quality. Audits
sample accepted and rejected work, and reviewers are evaluated on time and
accuracy as well as agreement.

## Protocol and Web3 Scoreboard

| Metric | Why it matters | Initial target |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Vault reconciliation difference | Detects accounting or indexer divergence | Exactly zero |
| Unfunded liability attempts | Confirms activation and obligation guards | Zero successful |
| Duplicate settlement | Tests replay and idempotency | Zero |
| Privileged single-key actions | Reveals unsafe authority | Zero on funded mainnet program |
| Indexer lag and divergence | Determines whether product state can be trusted | Alert threshold defined before pilot |
| Transaction completion | Shows real member/sponsor flow reliability | At least 98% excluding deliberate user cancellation |
| Median member signatures | Measures crypto friction | Minimize; target one understandable activation signature |
| Sponsored gas cost per active member | Tests AA economics | Within approved per-member budget |
| Public-chain value | Users/sponsors who say verification changed trust or operations | Qualitative plus interview count |
| Chain portability test | Confirms Robinhood dependency is isolated | Same core suite tests pass on a reference EVM environment |

If members refuse wallets or sponsors gain no value from public settlement,
Nakama should hide or remove crypto UX while preserving the best product. Web3
is justified by shared economic truth and portable authority, not by requiring
users to notice the chain.

## Token Scoreboard

Token metrics start only if the token-launch gate passes. They never enter the
PMF north star.

### Market health

- distribution excluding LP, treasury, team, and known related wallets
- holder concentration and beneficial-owner concentration where knowable
- liquidity depth and price impact at defined trade sizes
- realized volatility and drawdown
- volume quality, venue concentration, and suspected wash activity
- creator-fee income by source and concentration
- team, ecosystem, ACF, LP, and treasury vesting/unlock state

### Productive utility

- bonded operators/evaluators performing eligible work
- stake at risk for objective duties
- slashing or dispute events with documented process
- governance participation by eligible role, not raw wallet count
- grants producing accepted protocol or agent deliverables
- ACP jobs connected to the tokenized agent
- token actions that would be impossible or materially worse with stablecoins

A rising market capitalization with zero productive jobs is capital-market
attention. It is not token utility. Creator fees can fund runway, but they do
not prove sponsor demand or member benefit.

## Funnel Definitions

The commercial funnel uses strict stage exits:

```text
Named account
  -> qualified ICP: authority, exposure, deadline, budget path
  -> validated problem: incident/current alternative quantified
  -> design proposal: scope and price sent to decision-maker
  -> paid design partner: contract signed and cash collected
  -> funded pilot: complete budget and launch scope committed
  -> activated program: all gates pass and members can enroll
  -> completed program: obligations, refunds, reporting closed
  -> renewed/expanded/referred: costly repeat behavior
```

A social reply, warm introduction, demo, memorandum of understanding, or verbal
promise does not advance a revenue stage unless it satisfies the exit rule.

## Thirty-, Sixty-, and Ninety-Day Outcomes

### Days 0–30: prove the buyer problem

- 40 qualified target accounts researched
- 30 decision-maker interviews or a documented reason the segment cannot be
  reached
- at least 8 rank the problem top-three and at least 3 share useful data
- 10 priced design-sprint proposals
- at least 2 paid design partners
- one legal/product structure selected for a real sponsor context
- Robinhood testnet lifecycle and sponsor prototype demonstrated

### Days 31–60: earn a funded program

- one sponsor commits full pilot budget and signs subject to final gates
- exact cohort, terms, cap, operations, and member flow approved
- private evidence plane and accountable review rehearsal pass
- testnet program completes normal, appeal, refund, and incident paths
- contribution-margin model uses observed delivery time and vendor quotes
- token decision remains conditional and separate

### Days 61–90: activate only if safe

- contracts and operations pass independent review and release gates
- sponsor funds the segregated mainnet program vault
- eligible members complete moderated onboarding and comprehension tests
- program activates or receives a written no-launch decision with preserved
  evidence and next test
- agent sells at least one genuine external ACP job before marketplace utility
  is claimed
- token launches only if product, legal, Malaysia/Virtuals, technical, and
  treasury gates all pass; otherwise the product continues tokenless

These are outcome targets. The detailed backlog is in the implementation plan;
shipping documents or code without the commercial commitments does not satisfy
the period.

## Operating Cadence

### Daily during an active program

- reconcile vault balance, encumbrance, obligations, and settlements
- review urgent and approaching-SLA requests
- inspect agent-policy blocks and security alerts
- verify RPC, finality, indexer, wallet, paymaster, and evidence-service health
- assign one accountable owner to every exception

### Weekly before PMF

The founder, product, operations, protocol, and legal owners review one page:

1. New costly customer evidence and funnel stage changes.
2. Product and technical delivery against the funded-pilot critical path.
3. Legal, privacy, security, platform, or sponsor blockers.
4. Unit economics updated with actual cash and labor.
5. Decisions made, assumptions retired, and one highest-value next test.

Token price and social metrics appear in an appendix, not at the top of the
meeting. If the team spends more time explaining price than speaking to buyers,
the cadence is failing.

### Per-program reviews

- **Activation review:** terms, funding, roles, operations, technology, member
  communications, and go/no-go signatures.
- **Mid-program review:** service state, budget, member comprehension, incidents,
  agent errors, and required corrective actions.
- **Closure review:** final accounting, requests, appeals, unused budget,
  privacy/deletion state, sponsor/member feedback, economics, and renewal ask.
- **Independent sample review:** evidence access, decision quality, settlement,
  and public-report consistency.

### Monthly strategy review

The team rereads the four Arc questions, kill criteria, and decision register.
Every major hypothesis is marked supported, contradicted, unchanged, or
untested. Scope is added only when a current gate passes or a failed hypothesis
requires an explicit pivot.

## Metric Integrity Rules

- Define denominator and exclusion rules before looking at the result.
- Never combine legacy app users with protected program members.
- Never combine sponsor budget with Nakama revenue.
- Never annualize one cohort without labelling the assumption.
- Never count testnet, founder-funded, refunded, related-party, or free activity
  as independent paid demand.
- Never publish a wallet count as unique people without a defensible method.
- Never infer medical outcomes from claim frequency alone.
- Record missing data and failures; do not remove them to improve a rate.
- Freeze definitions for a program, then version changes prospectively.
- Link every public number to a reviewable evidence packet and permission.

## Decision Triggers

Metrics matter only when they change behavior:

- Fewer than 8 of 30 qualified buyers rank the problem top-three, or fewer than
  3 share useful data: reject or narrow the ICP.
- Fewer than 2 of 10 qualified proposals become paid design partners: stop
  custom build work and revisit problem, buyer, or offer.
- Member activation below 70%: diagnose trust, privacy, wallet, and relevance;
  remove crypto UX before blaming the cohort.
- Material decision error above 2% or appeal rate above 15%: do not expand agent
  authority; repair terms, evidence, and review.
- More than 20 staff hours to configure the third standard program: the
  platform thesis is not yet true.
- Fewer than 2 of the first 3 sponsors renew, expand, or make a qualified
  referral: behavior-change PMF remains unproven.
- No paid external ACP production job: remove marketplace and token-utility
  claims until behavior changes.
- No measurable customer or operating advantage from Robinhood after the
  pilot: preserve EVM portability and reassess the native-chain commitment.

The point of the system is to make continuation, narrowing, and stopping as
evidence-driven as launch.
