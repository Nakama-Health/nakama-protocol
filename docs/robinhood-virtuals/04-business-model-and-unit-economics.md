# Business Model and Unit Economics

## Economic Doctrine

Nakama needs a customer-funded operating business that remains viable if the
token trades poorly, creator fees fall to zero, and ACF never activates.

The model therefore separates four balance sheets:

1. **Program budget or claims-paying reserve:** assets legally and operationally
   available for member obligations.
2. **Nakama operating revenue:** fees earned for design, software,
   administration, and agent work.
3. **Protocol treasury:** funds for public infrastructure, security, grants, and
   ecosystem development.
4. **Token-market proceeds:** creator trading fees, ACF proceeds, or other
   launch-derived capital subject to Virtuals mechanics and legal review.

No dashboard, contract, accounting export, or marketing statement may merge
these categories.

## Buyer and Budget Owner

The first buyer is the community or cohort operator. Likely titles include:

- founder or executive director
- program or residency operator
- community lead with treasury authority
- people, benefits, or operations lead
- event or ecosystem program director

The member is an end user and may later become a contributor. The capital or
reserve partner is a separate counterparty. Treating all three as "the customer"
would obscure who can sign, pay, and renew.

## Initial Commercial Offer

### Protection Design Sprint

A fixed-scope paid engagement that converts a cohort problem into a launch or
no-launch recommendation.

Deliverables:

- incident and current-workflow analysis
- cohort and eligibility definition
- benefit and exclusion options
- budget, reserve, and exposure scenarios
- operating and data-flow design
- legal and partner question register
- member experience prototype
- implementation estimate and gate plan

Pricing hypothesis: `$2,500–$10,000`, creditable against a launch fee only when
that condition is agreed in advance.

### Genesis Program Launch

A one-time implementation fee for approved configuration, deployment,
integration, onboarding, runbooks, training, and launch support.

Pricing hypothesis: `$10,000–$25,000` plus pass-through legal, custody,
identity, payment, or specialist costs.

### Program Administration

A recurring fee for software, monitoring, member support, evidence workflows,
reporting, and controlled agent operations.

Pricing hypotheses to test:

- `$3–$10` per activated member per month
- a fixed cohort fee for short programs
- a minimum annual platform commitment for recurring organizations

Pricing must reflect the actual human work and liability profile. A low PMPM
fee with extensive bespoke review is worse than a higher transparent fee.

### Agent Services

Nakama can sell narrowly scoped jobs through its product and ACP:

- cohort protection readiness assessment
- program scenario generation
- reserve health report
- terms consistency audit
- evidence packet completeness review
- post-program audit report

Each job has a published input schema, output, exclusions, price, service level,
privacy classification, and refund behavior. Clinical adjudication and legal
advice are not generic marketplace jobs.

## Revenue Model by Stage

| Stage | Primary revenue | Secondary revenue | Excluded from base case |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Design partners | Paid design sprints | Prototype agent jobs | Token appreciation, ACF, reserve yield |
| Genesis launch | Setup and implementation fee | Cohort administration | Claims reserves as revenue |
| Repeat programs | Recurring platform/admin fee | Specialist-agent marketplace fee | Unapproved risk or capital fees |
| Network expansion | Operator software, protocol fees, marketplace fees | Legally supportable reserve/capital services | Guaranteed creator-fee income |

## Pilot Unit-Economics Template

Every proposal should model:

### Revenue

- design fee
- launch fee recognized over delivery
- recurring or cohort administration fee
- agent-job revenue
- pass-through costs separated from net revenue

### Direct delivery cost

- sales and solution design time
- legal and product review specific to the cohort
- identity and sanctions checks
- data storage and model inference
- member support
- evidence processing and human review
- payments and chain fees
- external evaluator or service-provider fees
- post-program reporting

### Capital and member obligations

- sponsor program budget in Phase 0; any later member contributions are a
  separate legal and product model
- backstop
- committed obligations
- approved unpaid amounts
- paid amounts
- unused and refundable funds

### Margin

Nakama contribution margin equals recognized operating revenue minus direct
delivery cost. Unused program budget and unpaid reserves are never margin.

## Illustrative Test Case

For a 100-member, 30-day sponsor-funded program, an initial proposal could test:

- `$7,500` paid design sprint
- `$15,000` launch and implementation fee
- `$5,000` cohort administration fee
- sponsor-posted program budget determined separately from Nakama fees
- pass-through legal, identity, payment, or clinical costs quoted separately

These figures are discovery anchors, not forecasts. The purpose is to learn
whether the buyer values the system enough to support the work required. If the
market accepts only a small fee while each cohort requires significant custom
legal and operating work, the direct-community model is not attractive.

## Venture-Scale Test

The model can become large through a combination of:

- recurring high-retention community programs
- templates that reduce launch cost and time
- distribution through aggregators, platforms, TPAs, insurers, or assistance
  networks
- operator and evaluator software fees
- agent commerce
- protocol usage across independently operated programs

It does not become venture-scale by collecting a small transaction fee on a few
bespoke pools.

An early warning threshold is:

> If expected annual contract value remains below approximately `$15,000`, the
> sales cycle exceeds 90 days, and delivery remains service-heavy, move upstream
> to aggregators or existing benefit operators rather than pretending direct
> community sales will scale.

This is a kill threshold to validate, not a valuation claim.

## Token-Derived Economics

Virtuals may route a share of trading fees to the creator and, under Unicorn,
may release ACF proceeds as FDV milestones are crossed. These flows should be
treated as volatile financing:

- model them at zero in the operating base case
- record gross receipts, token sales, conversion, taxes, and restrictions
- route them through a published treasury policy
- do not promise buybacks, distributions, yield, or revenue rights without
  specific legal analysis
- do not count them as program reserves unless a separately approved and
  segregated contribution is made
- do not time payroll, claims, or contractual promises around an FDV threshold

## ACF Use-of-Funds Policy

Before launch, Nakama should publish principles rather than rigid percentages:

1. Product, security, legal, and operating runway come first.
2. Funds support the mission and roadmap communicated at launch.
3. Any transfer into a member program is separately approved, disclosed, and
   segregated; it is never implied by the token launch.
4. Treasury decisions, related-party payments, and material deviations are
   reported.
5. ACF receipts do not create token-holder claims on the company, reserves, or
   future revenue.

An exact budget should follow actual proceeds, tax treatment, runway, and legal
advice rather than appearing as false precision in token marketing.

## Reserve Asset Economics

The Genesis program should favor liquidity and principal safety. Reserve yield
is not required for PMF and should be disabled initially unless:

- the asset and legal owner are explicit
- redemption is reliable in the operating jurisdictions
- duration and liquidity match expected obligations
- custody and smart-contract risk are acceptable
- losses and yield beneficiaries are disclosed
- the sponsor and members understand that yield does not increase guaranteed
  benefits unless terms say so

RWA positioning becomes legitimate when a specific tokenized legal asset,
issuer, custody, valuation, and redemption path is approved. Medical expenses
alone do not make `$NAKAMA` an RWA.

## Pricing Tests

Each qualified discovery call should test:

- current annual or per-cohort spend
- cost of previous incidents and staff time
- who owns the budget
- whether the buyer prefers fixed fee, PMPM, or bundled cohort pricing
- whether the sponsor will fund the complete Phase 0 budget and operating
  contingency; member or partner capital is tested only in a later structure
- the price at which approval changes from operational to executive
- whether a paid design phase is acceptable before launch
- which proof would justify renewal

Record the first price objection before negotiating. Repeated discounting can
hide a weak value proposition.

## Economic Success Gates

The first three programs should demonstrate:

- positive contribution margin or a documented path to it
- declining setup hours per new cohort
- no mixing of operating funds and program funds
- sponsor payment before custom delivery milestones
- direct delivery cost measured by function
- renewal or expansion from at least two of the first three sponsors
- agent automation reducing human work without increasing error or appeals

If those gates fail, token revenue should not be used to subsidize the illusion
of customer economics indefinitely.
