# Adversarial Review and Kill Criteria

## Adversarial Verdict

The vision is coherent, but the starting thesis originally stacked too many
unproven bets: a new protection structure, a new agent operating model, a new
chain, and a new token market. A polished launch could make that stack look
validated while none of the customer, legal, operating, or economic questions
had been answered.

The corrected plan keeps the ambition and reduces simultaneous uncertainty.
Nakama first sells and operates a sponsor-funded program for one bounded cohort.
It uses Robinhood Chain only where public economic state creates value. It
launches the productive agent before requiring a token. It launches `$NAKAMA`
only after independent product, platform, legal, technical, and treasury gates
pass.

This is not a conservative retreat. It creates a credible path from a real
customer outcome to autonomous mutual infrastructure rather than using the
category and token to skip the hard proof.

## The Four Stacked Bets

### 1. The customer bet

The assumed buyer is a community operator. The adversarial question is whether
that operator owns a material problem and budget, or merely likes the story.

Many online communities are audiences without a legal entity, operating team,
member identity, incident history, treasury authority, or willingness to carry
reputational responsibility. Their members may say portable health protection
is valuable while refusing to enroll, share evidence, use a wallet, or pay.

**Correction:** qualify named operators with repeated 50–250-person
international cohorts or comparable membership exposure, prior health
incidents, an upcoming program, an existing budget or spend, and authority to
fund the complete pilot.

### 2. The protection bet

The product may be regulated as insurance, a benefit, claims administration,
or another controlled activity despite different wording. Small cohorts can
also be economically unstable, subject to adverse selection, and expensive to
administer. A globally marketed promise can create jurisdictional exposure
before the product has operational proof.

**Correction:** Phase 0 uses sponsor funding, zero member price, a fixed
aggregate budget, a narrow schedule, named jurisdictions, accountable review,
and no claim that members are pooling risk. Member-funded or external-capital
structures wait for explicit legal and economic evidence.

### 3. The agent and protocol bet

Agents can reduce repetitive work, but the hardest work may remain human:
buyer qualification, term negotiation, legal coordination, evidence judgment,
fraud handling, difficult communication, appeals, and incident response. Public
settlement may increase complexity without changing sponsor or member trust.

**Correction:** measure staff time, error, override, appeal, and customer value
task by task. The agent has bounded tools and recommendation authority. The
third program must configure in under 20 hours of custom staff work or the
platform thesis is treated as unproven.

### 4. The chain and token bet

Robinhood Chain is new, operationally concentrated, dependent on external
infrastructure, and separate from Robinhood brokerage distribution. Virtuals
provides permissionless launch and agent commerce, but it does not guarantee
curation, volume, value, ACF, or legal permission. Current documentation and
live configuration disagree on several important mechanics. Malaysia
eligibility may block the route entirely.

**Correction:** preserve EVM portability, prefund onchain liquidity, isolate
Virtuals behind adapters, verify live contracts immediately before action, and
make the token a reversible launch decision after the product can operate
without it.

The category is Future Vision. The Hard Fact fit is medium because the target
segment's resignation to the status quo has not been measured. The problem is
not Hair on Fire for generic borderless communities; it can become Hair on
Fire for an operator with an upcoming cohort, prior incidents, and a budgeted
deadline.

## Strongest Arguments Against the Thesis

### Existing alternatives may be good enough

Sponsors can buy travel insurance, use an assistance provider, reimburse cases
from a discretionary budget, give stipends, or tell members to arrange their own
coverage. These options are imperfect but familiar, legally supported, and do
not ask members to understand wallets, stablecoins, agents, or a new category.

Nakama must show a concrete advantage in scope, transparency, speed,
administration, trust, reach, or economics. "Onchain" and "AI-native" are not
customer advantages until the buyer experiences the difference.

### A small pool can be worse than an insurer

Small cohorts have volatile exposure, limited diversification, thin operating
capacity, and difficult catastrophic risk. A transparent underfunded program is
still underfunded. Community trust can make a denial more personally damaging,
not less.

Nakama must cap Phase 0 to actual sponsor funding, avoid pretending a fixed
budget is insurance-grade risk transfer, and partner with regulated or
capitalized providers before it promises broader protection.

### Health data and public tokens create incompatible incentives

Speculators want frequent news, public metrics, and token utility. Members need
privacy, restrained communications, fair decisions, and no market pressure on
their case. A creator whose revenue rises with trading may optimize narrative
instead of operations. A sponsor who recovers unused budget may prefer denials.

Nakama must hard-separate data, decision rights, treasuries, and scoreboards.
No token proposal, market event, or sponsor instruction may decide an
individual outcome.

### Virtuals may be a distribution mirage

Permissionless launch lowers technical friction, not the threshold for trust.
Projects still need credible teams, useful products, sustained content,
liquidity, and buyers. A short-lived token spike can fund runway while damaging
the health product's legitimacy with sponsors and regulators.

Nakama must approach Virtuals with a working agent, a real product loop, a
diligence packet, and exact disclosures. If those assets are absent, waiting is
strategically stronger than launching a generic health-risk agent.

### The platform may remain services

Each sponsor may require unique jurisdictions, benefits, evidence rules,
contracts, communications, reviewers, and integrations. The agent may assist,
but Nakama could still be a consulting and operations company with software.

That is acceptable if priced honestly. It becomes a platform only when a new
program reuses reviewed templates, code, agent tools, workflows, reviewers, and
reporting with declining marginal staff time and errors.

## Pre-Mortem: July 2027

Assume the strategy failed one year after publication. The most likely story
is:

1. The team spent the first quarter perfecting contracts, token copy, and
   ecosystem relationships while sponsor discovery produced interest but no
   full budget.
2. `$NAKAMA` launched into attention, creator fees briefly rose, and market
   metrics became the team's daily priority.
3. The health offer remained legally ambiguous, so credible sponsors would not
   sign and public claims became increasingly vague.
4. A friendly community accepted a founder-funded demo, but activation was low
   because members did not understand the promise or did not want a wallet.
5. One complicated request required manual medical, legal, support, and payment
   work, exposing that the agent automated low-value drafting rather than the
   costly operating bottleneck.
6. The team called the demo traction and broadened into RWA, cyber, travel, and
   agent-risk narratives rather than closing the proof gap.
7. Token liquidity fell, the creator-fee runway disappeared, and neither a
   repeatable customer product nor durable token utility remained.

The plan prevents this outcome by putting cash, activation, delivery, renewal,
and independently useful agent jobs ahead of token milestones.

## Quantitative Kill Criteria

Kill criteria are commitments made before results are known. A kill can mean
stop, narrow, remove a component, or switch business model; it does not always
mean abandon Nakama.

| Test | Threshold | Required decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem discovery | After 30 qualified interviews, fewer than 8 rank the problem top-three or fewer than 3 share useful data | Reject or narrow the ICP; do not increase build scope |
| Paid design offer | After 10 qualified proposals, fewer than 2 pay a design fee/deposit | Stop custom product work and change buyer, problem, or offer |
| Full sponsor funding | No buyer commits the complete pilot budget after paid design work | Do not launch a protection program; sell planning/operations software if valued |
| Legal viability | Counsel finds no viable structure for the chosen entities/jurisdictions | Restrict to sponsor-funded support/navigation; no member pooling or risk-transfer claim |
| Member activation | Fewer than 70% of eligible sponsor-funded members activate | Remove wallet friction and retest trust/relevance before another launch |
| Member privacy/wallet rejection | More than 10% of eligible members refuse specifically because of wallet or privacy design | Replace the crypto-facing interaction; preserve onchain back office only if valuable |
| Clean-case service | Median clean-case decision exceeds 48 hours | Do not add cohorts until workflow and staffing are repaired |
| Decision quality | Appeal rate exceeds 15% or material error exceeds 2% over first 20 determinations | Freeze autonomy and expansion; revise terms, evidence, review, and staffing |
| Platform repeatability | Third standard program requires more than 20 custom staff hours to configure and contract | Treat product as services; reprice or move to channel/partner model |
| Retention | Fewer than 2 of first 3 sponsors renew, expand, or make a qualified referral | Product behavior change is unproven; do not broaden category |
| Direct unit economics | Expected ACV below about `$15,000`, sales cycle above 90 days, or persistent negative contribution margin under standard delivery | Move upstream to aggregators, TPAs, assistance networks, or licensed partners |
| Agent marketplace | No paid external production ACP job is delivered and accepted | Remove marketplace and token-utility claims; keep agent internal |
| Token necessity | Token utility is predominantly trading, governance theater, or circular staking | Do not launch, or stop expanding token-integrated product scope if already live |
| Robinhood advantage | Pilot shows no measurable distribution, UX, settlement, partner, or operating advantage relative to EVM alternatives | Keep EVM portability and reassess Robinhood as default chain |
| Platform permission | Virtuals/Malaysia eligibility or use-case permission remains unresolved | Do not launch or operate ACP through Virtuals |

## The Token No-Launch Decision

The token should not launch when any of these is true:

- no paid customer and no fully funded program
- no independently useful live agent
- no necessary token action that stablecoins, credentials, or contracts cannot
  perform more safely
- creator eligibility or Malaysia facts unresolved
- live allocation, fee, anti-sniper, ACF, vesting, or recipient mechanics not
  decoded and accepted
- public copy depends on price, promotion, volume, or implied Robinhood access
- team cannot operate treasury, market conduct, disclosures, security, and
  community support for at least twelve months
- token concentration or conflict would undermine sponsor/member trust

Missing a market window is cheaper than creating a permanent public asset with
unclear purpose and obligations.

## Strongest Alternative Strategy

If the protection structure is blocked but sponsors value the workflow, Nakama
should become **agentic member-support infrastructure** for cohort operators,
assistance providers, membership businesses, TPAs, and benefit administrators.

The product would provide:

- cohort eligibility and terms administration
- sponsor-funded assistance-budget controls
- private evidence intake and review workflow
- member navigation and communications
- auditable approvals and settlement instructions
- public or private aggregate reporting
- agent-assisted operations under partner authority

This alternative preserves most technology, the doctor-builder advantage, and
the community distribution thesis. It removes the claim that Nakama itself
creates a mutual or transfers risk. A licensed or capitalized partner can later
provide the regulated protection layer.

If customers value the service but not public settlement, keep the agent and
workflow and make blockchain a back-office option. If they value neither the
service nor settlement enough to pay, the correct action is to stop rather than
add RWA, DAO, or token narratives.

## Red-Team Questions Before Every Gate

### Before accepting a design partner

- Is the person a budget owner or a friendly connector?
- What happened recently that creates a deadline?
- What is spent today, by whom, and on which alternative?
- What costly action have they taken beyond a call?
- Would the problem remain urgent if Nakama used no blockchain or token?

### Before funding a program

- Which entity owes what to whom under which jurisdiction?
- Is the complete maximum promise funded with available assets?
- Who benefits economically from denial, delay, unused funds, or token price?
- Can a member understand and appeal a decision without the founder's help?
- What happens during chain, stablecoin, evidence-service, or reviewer failure?

### Before increasing agent authority

- Which measured task becomes faster, cheaper, or more accurate?
- What is the false-negative or harmful-error path?
- Can the tool reach data or money beyond the task?
- Does the human reviewer have time and incentive to challenge the model?
- Can the service continue safely if the model is unavailable?

### Before launching `$NAKAMA`

- What production action needs the token now?
- Which product evidence would still matter if price fell 90%?
- Are all creators/operators legally and contractually eligible?
- Has the exact live transaction been simulated and independently reviewed?
- Can the team explain allocations, fees, ACF, vesting, treasury, and risks in
  plain language without promising outcomes?
- Would a credible sponsor trust Nakama more after reading the launch page?

## Decision Rule

Continue when evidence retires a core uncertainty. Narrow when a specific ICP,
workflow, or channel works better than the general thesis. Pivot when customers
pay for an adjacent job but reject the proposed structure. Stop when repeated
costly tests fail and the only remaining positive signal is token or social
attention.
